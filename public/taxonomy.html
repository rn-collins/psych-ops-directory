The taxonomy

Any list can hold 175 names. What makes an index usable is knowing what a category means, what it deliberately excludes, and what an entry in it does not prove. All three are below, for all 23 categories — including the places where the taxonomy is defective.

How to read a category

Each entry below states three things. Includes is the rule that admitted a record. Excludes is the boundary — the neighbouring thing that looks similar and was filed elsewhere, which is usually more informative than the inclusion rule. A listing here does not tell you is the important one: it names, specifically, the questions a reader will want answered that this index cannot answer.

Categories are descriptive. They are not tiers, grades, or endorsements, and no ordering among them is implied. Earlier versions of this index carried tier labels; those were retired because a tier reads as a quality judgement and no quality judgement was ever made.

Known defects in this taxonomy

The taxonomy accreted during the survey rather than being designed in advance, and it has three defects. They are published rather than repaired, for a reason worth stating plainly: repairing a category assignment after the fact would mean filing records into categories no reviewer put them in, and a snapshot that quietly improves itself is no longer a record of what was found.

  1. Four category pairs cover the same ground.

    Banking and Banking & Financial Services; Capital Markets and Capital Markets / VC; Facilitator Training and Education & Training; Research Infrastructure and Research Collaborators. Each pair arose because the survey introduced a second label mid-way and never reconciled the first.

    How it is handled. Both labels are published as recorded, and each category page names its counterpart. Merging them now would silently reassign records to a category no reviewer put them in, and the whole point of a read-only snapshot is that it reports what was recorded.

  2. Two identifier prefixes are used by two different categories each.

    RC identifies both Research Collaborators and Retreat Centers; CM identifies both Capital Markets and Capital Markets / VC. An identifier prefix in this dataset therefore does not determine a category.

    How it is handled. Record identifiers are published as recorded and are unique within the set; only the prefix is ambiguous. Do not infer a category from an identifier.

  3. One category has a single member.

    Research Collaborators holds one record. A category of one is a filing decision rather than a class.

    How it is handled. Published as recorded, and labelled as such on the taxonomy page.

Professional services an organisation in this field buys

Legal 20 records

Includes. Law firms and individual attorneys whose published practice description includes psychedelics, entheogens or the state programmes regulating them.

Excludes. General-practice firms with no published psychedelic practice, and non-lawyer consultants who advise on regulation.

A listing here does not tell you. Nothing about competence, bar standing, malpractice history, current client conflicts, or whether the firm still does this work. Attorneys move; three entries here describe practices that migrated to another firm listed in the same category.
