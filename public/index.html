About This Directory

What this is

Psych Ops Directory is not a directory of retreat centers, therapists, or facilitators — plenty of those already exist. This is the operational back-end: the legal counsel, banks and credit unions, clinical research organizations, insurers, staffing agencies, and grant administrators that a psychedelic-adjacent organization needs in order to actually operate under the rules that govern it. Current counts by listing and category are in the header stats above — each entry is editorially reviewed rather than pulled from a generic business database.

Why it exists

Legal, banking, and clinical infrastructure for this ecosystem is scattered, and generic "psychedelic business" search results mix real field experience with law firms and vendors chasing a trend. Someone standing up a licensed service center, a research protocol, or a facilitator training program needs to know which counsel actually drafted the statute they're operating under, which bank will still hold their account in a year, and which vendor's "psychedelic experience" is one named client versus a genuine practice. That's the gap this directory is built to close.

How listings are vetted

Every listing is scored into one of three tiers, shown throughout the directory (see legend above the grid). Tier 1 means a documented, named psychedelic relationship — a specific public client, case, license, or publication documented during editorial review. Tier 2 means verifiable field presence without a confirmed named relationship — the organization operates in adjacent space (cannabis law, behavioral health staffing) with credible but less direct psychedelic-specific evidence. Tier 3 is a candidate listing that should be independently verified before you rely on it — usually because a firm's psychedelic practice has changed hands, a founder has departed, or public information is thin. Editor notes flag anything time-sensitive, such as attorneys who moved firms or practices that have substantially wound down.

How to use it

Search by name, service, or specialty, or browse by category. Select multiple listings and use Compare for a side-by-side view during due diligence, or Export CSV to take the current result set into your own vendor-evaluation process. If you know a vendor that belongs here, use Submit Listing — every submission is reviewed by RN Collins before it's added. If something looks outdated or wrong, use the flag icon on that listing so it can be corrected or removed. This directory is maintained on an editorial review cycle, not scraped automatically, which means it changes more slowly than the ecosystem itself — treat the flag mechanism as the fastest way to keep it accurate.

Privacy

Search, filter, comparison, and export require no account. Aggregate events—including page views, searches, categories, comparisons, and exports—may be recorded to improve the directory. Do not put personal, medical, confidential, or privileged information into search fields.

Listing submissions and flags store what you enter, including an email when supplied, for editorial review and follow-up. Information is not sold or used for behavioral advertising. Due-diligence notes remain in this browser’s local storage unless you separately submit them. Use Clear saved diligence or browser controls to remove them.

Contact the Architect opens an email draft in your mail application; the site does not claim a message was sent. Privacy or deletion requests: rncollins@antithesis.law.

Process verified 15 August 2026

Corrections and removal

Use Flag on the relevant listing and identify what is inaccurate, outdated, unsafe, duplicated, closed, or no longer authorized. For removal, privacy, or identity-sensitive requests, email rncollins@antithesis.law with the listing ID, requested action, supporting source, and your relationship to the record. Do not send confidential client information.

Requests enter the editorial review queue. The editor checks the cited source, current provider or regulator record where applicable, and tier criteria before correcting, downgrading, annotating, or removing a listing. A materially disputed record may be marked unverified during review.
